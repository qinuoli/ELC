After three days of dry heat, a slow evening rain moved across the city just before sunset. The first drops darkened the pavement in narrow streaks, and within minutes the main road reflected the traffic lights in long red and green lines.

People changed their pace immediately. Some stepped under the bus stop roof, while others continued walking with bags held above their heads. Street vendors covered their tables with plastic sheets and waited for the rain to settle into a more regular pattern.

The sound was more noticeable than the wind. Water tapped on metal railings, storefront awnings, and bicycle seats left outside apartment entrances. From an upper-floor window, the entire block seemed quieter, not because fewer people were present, but because everyone moved with more attention.

By the time the rain ended, the air was cooler and the streets were brighter under the lamps. The city looked briefly washed and simplified, as if the weather had cleared away a layer of noise without changing the structure beneath it.
